Build a website for a studio that sells vibe coding as a service — people come with an idea, a mood, a half-sentence scribbled on a napkin, and leave with working software that was conjured, not engineered.

This is a creativity test. Who the studio is, how it talks, what it believes, what it sells and for how much, what the visitor sees, touches and feels — all of it is yours to invent. The site itself should feel like proof of the craft: if this studio can vibe something into existence, its own website is the first exhibit.

Only a few things are fixed. Everything else is your call.

**Eight blocks.** The site has exactly eight blocks. What they are, what they are called, what order they come in and how the visitor moves between them is up to you. The one thing the eight must do together is sell: somewhere in there the studio offers its services, and a visitor who wants to hire it can tell what they would get and how to reach out.

**Real 3D.** The site must contain real-time 3D, and it has to matter. Something you built yourself that belongs to this studio's world and responds to the visitor — not a stock shape rotating behind a headline. If the 3D could be deleted without the site losing its meaning, it is decoration; rethink it.

**Generated images.** Use your image generation tool to create original images for the site, with one art direction that clearly comes from the same world as everything else on the page. Make them part of the design rather than pictures dropped into rectangles. No stock photos, no hotlinked images, no placeholders, nothing drawn in code and passed off as an image. If you have no image generation tool here, say so before you start building.

**Loud color.** Bright, saturated, confident color that owns large areas of the screen. A dark page with one neon accent is not bright, and neither is a white page with one brand color or anything pastel.

**Digital style.** The aesthetic of the whole site is digital: technological, modern, futuristic — the visual language of tech culture and digital art, with graphics, motion and interaction that all feel high-tech. Not corporate, not cozy, not vintage. Find your own take on that style rather than reaching for the stock cyberpunk kit.

It must be creative and genuinely unusual. Not "a nice landing page with a twist" — a thing with its own logic, its own world, its own rules. Something that makes a person stop, poke at it, and send the link to a friend with "look at this". The structure, the layout, the way it moves, the way it reads — none of it should follow the shape you'd reach for by default. If you catch yourself building what a website is "supposed" to look like, stop and go somewhere stranger.

Hard rule: no AI slop. You know exactly what it looks like — violet-to-blue gradients, glowing blobs, glassmorphism cards, bento grids, sparkle and rocket emojis, Inter on a dark background, "Unleash the power of…", "Transform your ideas into reality", wireframe spheres floating for no reason, copy that could belong to any company on earth. The same goes for the images: no glossy robots, no glowing brains, no smooth over-rendered look that marks a picture as generated. If a detail could appear on a thousand other AI-generated sites, it doesn't belong on this one. Every word and every pixel should feel chosen by someone with taste and an opinion.

Have a point of view. Take risks. Be weird on purpose, not by accident. Make it something no one has seen before — and if it reminds you of something you've seen a thousand times, throw it out and start over.

Deliver it as a site that opens from `index.html` in this folder and runs locally with no build step. Before you call it done, open it in a real browser, look at it critically, and fix whatever looks default, broken or unfinished.
